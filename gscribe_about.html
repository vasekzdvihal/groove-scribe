About Groove Scribe

Groove Scribe helps drummers create, practice and share grooves.

  • A simple grid makes sheet music that you can print and share.
  • MIDI playback with tempo and a metronome helps you hear and practice hard grooves.
  • A link shares a groove. The other person sees it in any web browser.

This is a personal fork. It is based on the original Groove Scribe by Lou Montulli. This fork adds Google sign-in and a library of lessons and grooves. It also adds offline use and changes for tablets. The original project is at github.com/montulli/GrooveScribe. The source of this fork is at github.com/vasekzdvihal/groove-scribe.

How it works

Groove Scribe is written in HTML and JavaScript. It runs in the browser. ABC Notation makes the printable sheet music. When you change a note, the page writes new ABC Notation and draws the music.

To play the music, Groove Scribe makes a MIDI file and plays it with the browser audio. The sounds are custom drum samples from Mike Johnston's kits.

The icons and images are SVG or fonts. They stay sharp at any size. Zoom in on the page to see.

How it swings

The swing slider changes the playback. It does not change the printable sheet music. To make swing, the page delays the upbeat notes in the measure. The upbeat notes are the "a" and the "e" in a 16th note groove (1e&a). In an 8th note groove they are the "&" notes (1&2&3&4&).

When the page makes the MIDI file, it makes the downbeat notes longer and the upbeat notes shorter. The swing percentage sets how much the upbeat is delayed: NewDelay = NormalDelay + (NormalDelay * SwingPercentage).
